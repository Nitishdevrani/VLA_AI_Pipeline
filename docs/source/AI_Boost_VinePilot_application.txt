3. Problem Fit & Contribution
Explain how the solution addresses the challenge and benefits key stakeholders while aligning with industry needs.
Every new vineyard mission needs a ROS programmer. VinePilot replaces that with a language layer an operator can
use and an integrator can audit. A grower says “check the third row and tell me which clusters look ripe.” Streaming
ASR returns text with per-span confidence; an instruction-tuned LLM resolves intent against an explicit catalogue of
robot actions and emits a typed plan: navigate(row=3) -> survey(target=“ripe cluster”) -> report(map_overlay)
- never raw motor commands. Perception is steered by the same language: our ECCV 2026 oral paper “Steerable
Visual Representations” uses gated cross-attention to make a frozen ViT respond to the mission phrase, so “ripe
cluster”, “trunk” or “obstacle” each redirect one backbone rather than a detector per concept. A topological row
map and live pose ground every spatial reference. A deterministic validator checks types, preconditions, geofences,
confidence and safety rules before compiling to ROS1 actions; uncertain or safety-critical plans go to a human. This
covers all five challenge objectives - speech to text, LLM intent and task decomposition, executable ROS plans, visual
and map context, platform validation - and is measured throughout against the Challenge Owner’s GUI/manual
baseline. Growers gain access without scripting and integrators gain an inspectable interface extended by editing a
schema, without retraining a policy; the Challenge Owner gains a VLA proof of concept transferable to inspection
and maintenance.

4. Quantifiable Outcomes
Define KPIs, benchmarks and measurable improvements.
All targets follow the Challenge Owner’s protocol (70/15/15 split, 3-5 paraphrases per mission, GUI/manual baseline)
and are submitted monthly to the automatic-scoring leaderboard. KPI1, intent and slot accuracy >=92% (target
>=90%): grammar-constrained decoding removes malformed-plan errors, so residual error is semantic, not syntactic.
KPI2, plan correctness and mission completion >=87% in simulation and >=85% on the physical platform (target
>=85%). KPI3, >=87% success across 3-5 paraphrases per mission, reporting the worst paraphrase rather than the
mean. KPI4, median end-to-end latency <=5 s and p95 <=10 s, from speech onset to first ROS goal. Safety: 100% of
executed plans pass schema, capability, precondition and geofence validation; zero unvalidated model output reaches
ROS; >=95% correct refusal on a held-out suite of unsafe, out-of-scope and prompt-injection instructions, reported
with the false-refusal rate so abstention cannot be gamed. Perception: grounding F1 and mask IoU per stratum
(cultivar, colour, growth stage, lighting, occlusion), <=5-point worst-group gap where data supports the stratum,
plus expected calibration error. Efficiency: under 1% trainable parameters and >=40% lower adapter-training energy
than end-to-end VLA fine-tuning (OpenVLA / pi-zero class), measured on identical hardware with our Robot Control
Stack harness. Usability: >=50% lower median mission-authoring time than the GUI baseline, over >=5 operators
and >=10 missions.

5. Innovation & Novelty
Describe novelty relative to existing approaches and how the solution overcomes current limitations.
Three claims separate VinePilot from current practice. (1) Steer vision with language before planning. Monolithic
VLAs (RT-2, OpenVLA, pi-zero) and LLM-planner stacks (SayCan, Code-as-Policies) both read a visual encoder
fixed before the mission is known. Our ECCV 2026 oral paper “Steerable Visual Representations” injects gated
cross-attention into a frozen ViT so the mission phrase determines which evidence is encoded, training only 21M
multimodal parameters. One backbone attends to small, non-salient clusters, trunks, row ends or obstacles with no
detector per concept. (2) Generate a plan, not an action. The LLM emits a typed, human-readable mission DSL
under grammar-constrained decoding; a deterministic compiler binds every symbol to a whitelisted ROS1 capability
with preconditions, ordering and geofences. Code-as-Policies emits code whose only check is running it; we make
the checkable artefact the output, with evidence and calibrated confidence per step. (3) Adapt by editing schemas.
New crops, actions or robots enter through the capability catalogue, prompt vocabulary and a small adapter, not a
retrained policy. The result is a compute-efficient VLA stack whose failure modes are inspectable.

6. Technology Readiness
Describe the current TRL and justify the ability to reach TRL5.
The integrated system is TRL2; its hardest component is already validated. Text-steerable visual representation
is published with code and checkpoints (ECCV 2026 oral). We also hold working code for multimodal grounding
(TWIST & SCOUT; PIN), parameter-efficient adaptation and compression (VeRA; The LLM Surgeon), compact
long-CoT SFT, robustness under domain shift, and 3D/spatial pre-training. What does not exist is the integrated
mission generator on the Challenge Owner’s ROS1 platform - precisely the Advance Phase work. Route to TRL5,
mapped to the competition calendar: by the December 2026 Mid-Term Checkpoint, speech-or-text to validated ROS1
plan executing in containerised simulation, with task-conditioned grape perception, safety validation and the first
leaderboard submissions; January 2027, integration on the physical platform in controlled conditions; February 2027,
repeated end-to-end trials in the JOiiNT LAB vineyard row on unseen instructions and live outdoor imagery. TRL5
evidence is the logged record of those trials against KPI1-4, the GUI/manual baseline, safety events, latency traces
and per-stratum perception results, plus a reproducible container and the Final Report. Integration risk is reduced
three ways: backbones stay frozen, so no large training run sits on the critical path; platform interfaces are mirrored
as simulator stubs from month one; and the capability catalogue is co-designed with the Challenge Owner from
kick-off rather than inferred later.

7. AI Technology & Justification
Detail the AI architectures and complementary technologies, and explain their efficiency, accuracy and scalability.
Five independently testable modules. (1) Speech: locally hosted streaming multilingual ASR (Whisper-class) with
word-level confidence and a lexicon of cultivar and row names; low-confidence spans trigger clarification. (2) Planning:
an open-weight 7-14B instruction model (European candidates prioritised), 4-bit QLoRA (or our more efficient VeRA
variant) on verified mission traces, paraphrases, clarifications and negative safety examples. Grammar-constrained
decoding emits a typed mission DSL of goals, entities, spatial references, preconditions and recovery. The model
calls tools instead of inventing state: capabilities, pose, map, perception. (3) Perception: SteerViT from our ECCV
2026 oral paper - gated cross-attention over a frozen DINOv3-class ViT makes patch and global features respond to
the mission phrase; a head returns masks, map-frame coordinates and calibrated confidence. Only 21M multimodal
parameters are post-trained: new vocabulary costs GPU-hours, not weeks. (4) Grounding: a topological row graph
(row IDs, entry, exit, headland) over a metric occupancy and semantic layer from the provided maps, resolving “the
third row” or “the row end”. (5) Validation and execution: a deterministic parser, capability-graph planner and
ROS1 compiler check the DSL against action signatures, preconditions, ordering and geofences, then emit ROS1
goals; a runtime monitor compares expected with observed state and triggers clarification, replanning or safe stop.
The principle: what changes (prompts, schemas, adapters) sits where change is cheap; what is safety-critical sits
where behaviour is provable. Edge feasibility: frozen backbones, 4-bit weights, cached text embeddings, selective
crops. Training uses the provided grape data, licence-audited WGISD, AI4Agriculture and wGrapeUNIPD-DL,
synthetic paraphrases and simulation traces; final numbers use held-out vineyard data. Containerised ROS1 nodes
with versioned schemas decouple models from hardware, easing a ROS2 port.

8. Trustworthy, Responsible AI & Compliance
Address robustness, transparency, explainability, human oversight, bias mitigation and EU regulatory alignment.
The generative model is advisory; a deterministic layer decides what executes. It cannot publish arbitrary ROS
commands: every step must match a typed schema and a whitelisted capability, satisfy preconditions, stay inside
geofences and pass collision and operational checks. Human approval is mandatory before first execution of a new
mission, after material plan changes, and whenever ASR, perception or planning confidence falls below calibrated
thresholds. Runtime monitoring, pause, safe stop and the physical emergency stop are independent of the model.
Every mission stores instruction, transcript, retrieved state, perception evidence, structured plan, validation result,
operator decision and execution trace, so any behaviour can be replayed and attributed. Because the plan is a typed
DSL, the interface explains the intended action sequence in plain language and flags assumptions and unresolved
references before, not after, motion. Robustness testing covers paraphrase, accent and speaking-rate variation,
prompt injection, out-of-distribution scenes, occlusion, lighting, weather and sensor dropout, and deliberately unsafe
instructions; we report calibration and worst-group performance and prefer abstention, clarification and conservative
fallback plans to forced predictions. On regulation: an agricultural mobile robot is machinery, so we will run the
classification jointly under the AI Act and Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 with the Challenge Owner. Competition work
falls under the AI Act research and testing provisions, but we will build to the high-risk obligations regardless - risk
management, data governance, logging, human oversight, accuracy and cybersecurity - and structure the safety case
using ISO 12100 hazard analysis. GDPR, dataset licences and machinery safety obligations are documented in model
and system cards delivered with the Final Report.

9. Ethics & Data Governance
Explain ethical data handling, GDPR compliance, privacy by design, integrity and bias mitigation.
A data register records source, licence, permitted use, provenance, checksums, annotations, transformations, model
access and retention for every asset. WGISD (CC BY-NC 4.0), AI4Agriculture (MIT), wGrapeUNIPD-DL (CC BY
4.0) and Challenge Owner data are stored separately where licences differ; no output is relicensed beyond source
permissions, and the WGISD non-commercial clause is tracked through to which checkpoints it touches. The challenge
imagery contains no personal data. Voice does: operator recordings are personal data, handled with informed consent,
a documented lawful basis, local processing where practical, purpose limitation, short retention and deletion on
request. Raw audio is discarded once the transcript is verified; a DPIA and registration with UTN’s data protection
officer precede any operator session. Access is role-based and encrypted in transit and at rest; experiment logs avoid
unnecessary identifiers. The Challenge Owner’s 70/15/15 split is the primary protocol; we additionally report a
scene-grouped split to quantify near-duplicate leakage, since consecutive vineyard frames overlap heavily. Dataset
and model cards report class balance, cultivar, lighting, season and annotation uncertainty. Stratified evaluation,
targeted augmentation, reweighting and confidence calibration mitigate the environmental and red-grape imbalance
the Challenge Owner flags. Synthetic language and mission data are labelled as such and validated before SFT.

Impact
1. Sustainability & Scalability AI
Explain continued impact, retraining, ethical reuse, community engagement, compliance and adaptation.
VinePilot is designed as a maintained system, not a one-off model. Stable interfaces separate speech, planning,
perception, safety validation and ROS execution, so new crops, actions or robots arrive through versioned capability
schemas, prompt vocabularies and small adapters rather than a full retrain. The visual module freezes its backbone
and trains only text-conditioning layers; the planner uses PEFT, 4-bit quantization and compact verified SFT sets.
That keeps update cost low enough that maintenance survives the end of the prize funding, and keeps inference
feasible on the robot. Deployment monitoring tracks drift in intent accuracy, perception, calibration, latency and
mission success against thresholds agreed with the Challenge Owner; a documented trigger policy initiates retraining
when those degrade, while a model registry, dataset lineage, regression tests and rollback preserve accountability. Data
reuse follows original licences, purpose limitation and the register; voice data is not retained by default. European
open-weight models keep the stack deployable without dependence on a non-EU API provider - which matters for
farms and SMEs that cannot send field imagery to a third party. We will engage fully with the AI-BOOST programme
- kick-off, CINECA onboarding and training, monthly leaderboard submissions, Mid-Term Checkpoint, technical
webinars and the Brussels final - and co-design mission vocabulary and explanations with JOiiNT LAB, integrators
and operators. Reproducible protocols, interfaces and non-sensitive code will be published where the Participation
Agreement and six-month exclusivity permit, and disseminated through ELLIS and our CVPR/ICCV workshop
series. Beyond the competition, maintenance is sustained through joint research, integration licences and follow-on
public-private projects; the typed planner and steerable perception layer carry over to inspection and other field
robotics with domain-specific safety rules swapped in.

3. Fairness, Diversity & Open Science
Describe gender, diversity, inclusion, stakeholder engagement and open-science practices.
Two datasets carry fairness risk. Speech data involves people: operator recordings follow a coverage plan spanning
gender, age, speaking rate, regional accent and non-native English, since ASR error rates vary along these axes
and a misheard command becomes a wrong mission. We report intent and transcription accuracy by stratum,
aggregated where samples are too small to protect participants, and cap the worst-group gap rather than optimising
the mean. The imagery involves the vineyard: the Challenge Owner flags over-representation of red grapes and of
particular lighting and seasons, so we audit coverage across cultivar, colour, growth stage, occlusion, weather and
row geometry, publish disaggregated metrics, and direct new collection at weak strata. Inclusion is also an interface
property: text is a first-class input alongside speech, clarification replaces silent failure, and plans are in plain language.
Operator sessions draw on growers, technicians and safety stakeholders, not only engineers. delivery team spans
PhD, postdoctoral and professorial career stages, and new research-associate posts follow UTN’s equal-opportunity
recruitment. Open-science outputs, subject to dataset licences, security review and the six-month exclusivity, will
include non-sensitive code, the mission schema, evaluation scripts, model and data cards, energy measurements and
benchmark definitions, released FAIR with DOIs. Negative results and limitations are documented alongside headline
KPIs.

4. Exploitation and Potential for Collaboration
Explain exploitation and scaling beyond TRL5, cross-sector adoption, open research and public-private partnerships.
After TRL5 we will harden VinePilot as a vendor-neutral mission-generation layer with three separable assets: a
typed mission DSL with its validator and compiler, a prompt-steerable perception service, and ROS1 adapters
with a ROS2 migration path. Each can be adopted independently, which lowers the barrier for an integrator who
already has a navigation stack. Intellimech and JOiiNT LAB are the primary exploitation partners: together we
will capture integrator requirements during the Advance Phase, validate the vineyard use case, and define a route
to pilots with agricultural-robotics SMEs through Intellimech’s network of more than seventy member enterprises.
We accept the six-month foreground exclusivity for the Challenge Owner; subsequent exploitation and licensing
follow the Participation Agreement and each component’s background-IP and dataset-licence constraints, which we
have already mapped per module. UTN’s technology transfer office will assess protectable elements; the lead holds
three granted patents and eight pending deep-learning filings, so this route is realisable. Cross-sector transfer reuses
the core stack with capability schemas and safety rules replaced: industrial inspection, infrastructure maintenance,
greenhouse and orchard operations, and autonomous field logistics all share the structure of “grounded language in,
validated plan out”. Scientific exploitation includes evaluation protocols for steerable perception in embodied tasks,
safety-constrained LLM planning, and compute-aware VLA adaptation; these will be submitted to CVPR, CoRL or
ICRA and disseminated through the BigMAC and LIMIT workshop series we organise and through ELLIS. Subject

5. Risk Management & Mitigation
Identify the key implementation risks and their mitigations.
Robot access and distance (high): team in Nuremberg, platform in Bergamo. Interface specs in M1, platform
mirrored in simulation, on-site visits, remote execution and log replay between visits. ROS1/hardware mismatch
(high): contract tests against stubs, a named ROS1 lead, integration from M4, weekly reviews; ROS1 is end-of-
life, so nodes ship containerised with pinned dependencies. Hallucination or unsafe decomposition (high): typed
constrained output, whitelisted capabilities, deterministic precondition and geofence checks, adversarial tests, human
approval, independent stops. Physical safety (high): controlled trials, speed and workspace limits, safe stop and
emergency stop; no autonomous outdoor run without prior approval. Visual domain shift (cultivar, occlusion, lighting,
weather; medium-high): stratified testing, augmentation, calibration, abstention, targeted collection. Speech or
reference ambiguity (medium): ASR confidence, map-based entity resolution, clarification first. Latency or edge
limits (medium): frozen backbones, adapters, quantization, caching, fallback missions. Data leakage or licence conflict
(medium): provenance register, scene-grouped split alongside the mandated one, licence-separated storage, audit.
Automatic-scoring mismatch (medium): treat the leaderboard metric as a first-class target, clarified at kick-off.
Schedule/personnel (medium): modular packages, two-deep ownership, monthly gates, pre-agreed scope cuts if one
slips.
